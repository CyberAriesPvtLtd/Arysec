What Internal Audit Actually Owes You

Internal audit sits in a different place from the two things it is most often confused with. The statutory audit is bounded by company law and speaks to shareholders. A penetration test asks whether a particular system can be broken into on a particular day. Internal audit answers a broader question for your board: are the controls management says exist actually designed correctly and operating over a period, across finance, operations, technology and compliance.

We provide that function on an outsourced basis, where we are your internal audit team, or co-sourced, where we work alongside your in-house auditors and bring the technology and analytics skills that are hardest to recruit. Either way the work is planned against risk rather than habit. The annual plan is built from your risk register, your regulatory obligations, prior findings and the places where the organisation has recently changed — a new ERP, an acquisition, a move to cloud hosting, a large outsourcing arrangement.

Our methodology follows the Institute of Internal Auditors' Global Internal Audit Standards and tests controls against the COSO Internal Control Integrated Framework, with COBIT 2019 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A applied where the subject matter is technology. Evidence discipline separates a useful audit from a comfortable one. Inquiry alone proves nothing, so we inspect, reperform and sample, we test the completeness of the populations we sample from, and every conclusion in the report traces back to a working paper a reviewer can follow.

Every review produces a documented trail your audit committee, your statutory auditor and your regulator can follow without asking us to explain it.

  • Risk and control matrix for each process in scope
  • Detailed audit programme with test steps and sample rationale

  • Findings register with owners, dates and ageing analysis
  • Quarterly audit committee pack and progress summary
  • Follow-up testing memo confirming closure of prior findings

Capabilities

What the Function Covers

Risk-Based Annual Plan

We map your audit universe, score each entity by inherent risk and control maturity, and agree a plan the audit committee approves, with capacity reserved for management requests and unplanned reviews.

ITGC Testing

Access, change and operations controls get tested across your ERP, operating systems and databases: joiner and leaver processing, privileged accounts, segregation of duties conflicts, change approval and migration, job scheduling, and backup restoration.

Information Security Controls

Security controls are assessed against ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A or NIST CSF 2.0: identity and access management, logging and monitoring, vulnerability management, cloud configuration, third-party assurance and incident handling.

Business Process Reviews

Procure to pay, order to cash, payroll and inventory are walked end to end. We test authorisation limits, three-way matching, master data changes and manual journals, then report control gaps alongside process inefficiency.

Data Analytics Testing

Where the data supports it we test whole populations rather than samples: duplicate payments, vendor and employee bank account matches, journals posted outside working hours, and access combinations that breach segregation rules.

Findings and Follow-Up

Each issue is written with root cause, risk rating and a management action agreed with a named owner and a date. Follow-up testing verifies the fix before anything is marked closed.

How It Works

Our Engagement Process

How a Internal Audit engagement runs from first conversation to steady state.

01

Plan

We build the audit universe from your entity structure, systems, processes and regulatory obligations, score it for risk, and take a proposed plan and audit charter to your audit committee for approval.

02

Scope

Each review opens with a terms of reference: objectives, processes, systems, locations, period covered and the controls to be tested. Walkthroughs with process owners confirm how the control actually runs before testing begins.

03

Test

Design is assessed first, then operating effectiveness over the period, using inspection, reperformance and sampling sized to control frequency. Observations are discussed with owners as we go, so nothing in the draft report surprises anyone.

04

Report

Findings go to management with ratings, root cause and agreed actions, then to the audit committee in a pack that shows plan progress, thematic issues and overdue items. Follow-up testing closes them.

Internal Audit — Common Questions

Different objective, different audience. Your statutory auditor forms an opinion on whether the financial statements give a true and fair view, works to a scope set by law and reports to shareholders. Internal audit reports to your audit committee on whether controls across operations, technology and compliance work, including areas that never touch the financial statements — access management, cloud configuration and vendor governance. The two are complementary, and under the standards on using the work of internal auditors, good internal audit evidence can reduce your statutory auditor's testing.

No. A penetration test tries to break into a system and reports what an attacker could reach on the day it ran. An IT audit asks whether the controls meant to prevent that were designed properly and operated across the whole period: whether every leaver lost access within the stated timeframe, whether changes reached production with approval, whether alerts were reviewed. The pen test finds the unpatched server. The audit explains why patching failed and who owns the fix. We use pen test results as evidence within an audit.
