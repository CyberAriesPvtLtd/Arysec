From Raw Logs to Answered Alerts

Most organisations that buy monitoring already own the tools. The gap is rarely the platform; it is the operating discipline around it. A security operations centre is people, use cases, runbooks and shift handovers as much as it is a console. Where that discipline is missing, the queue fills with thousands of events a day, the same benign pattern fires dozens of times a week, and analysts learn to close alerts quickly rather than read them. That is alert fatigue, and it is how a genuine intrusion gets dismissed as noise. Our first job on any monitoring engagement is to make the queue small enough that every item in it deserves a human.

Detection begins with what we can actually see. We inventory log sources against the events that matter: authentication, endpoint process execution, DNS, proxy and firewall traffic, cloud control plane activity, identity provider records and the applications holding your sensitive data. Gaps are closed before a single rule is written. Ingestion is normalised to a common schema so that a username means the same thing whether it arrived from Active Directory, Entra ID or a Linux host. Detection content is then engineered use case by use case and mapped to MITRE ATT&CK techniques, which gives you a coverage picture instead of a rule count. You can see that credential access is well covered, that lateral movement is thin, and decide where the next month of engineering effort should go.

Monitoring only pays off if the network underneath it is arranged to contain an intruder. We review firewall and next-generation firewall policy for the rules nobody is willing to remove, test whether your segmentation actually holds, and design zones and micro-segmentation around the systems that would hurt most: payment environments, core banking interfaces, operational technology, build infrastructure and the jump hosts administrators log in from. Zero-trust network access replaces flat VPN reachability with per-application authorisation tied to user identity and device posture. Intrusion detection and prevention sensors go where east-west traffic is visible, not only at the perimeter. None of this replaces your staff. Most of our clients run a co-sourced model in which we hold the overnight watch, the detection engineering and the tuning backlog, while their internal team keeps ownership of the estate and the final call on major incidents.

Capabilities

How We Run Detection

SIEM Deployment and Tuning

We deploy or take over your SIEM, whether that is Microsoft Sentinel, Splunk, Elastic, Wazuh or QRadar, then spend the first weeks suppressing known-benign patterns and rewriting rules that fire without cause.

Log Ingestion and Use Cases

Sources are onboarded, parsed and normalised, then detection use cases are written for the threats relevant to your sector and estate, each documented with its logic, data dependency and expected analyst action.

Endpoint Detection and Response

EDR and XDR agents are deployed, policy-tuned and monitored so that process, registry and memory activity is visible. Containment actions such as host isolation are pre-agreed, so nobody waits for permission at 3am.

Analyst-Led Alert Triage

Every alert reaching the queue is investigated by an analyst against a documented runbook, enriched with asset and identity context, then closed with a recorded reason or escalated to your named contact.

Threat Hunting

Hypothesis-led hunts run on a scheduled cadence against MITRE ATT&CK techniques your detections do not yet cover, using current threat intelligence and behaviours seen in your own environment rather than indicator lists alone.

Firewall and Segmentation Review

Rule bases are audited for shadowed, permissive and unused entries, then segmentation is designed and tested: VLAN and zone boundaries, micro-segmentation for critical workloads, and zero-trust access replacing broad VPN reachability.

How It Works

Our Engagement Process

How a Network Security engagement runs from first conversation to steady state.

01

Map Coverage

We review your current tooling, log coverage, alert volumes and existing rules, then interview the people who handle alerts today. The output is an honest picture of what is monitored and what is not.

02

Onboard

Sources are connected and normalised, agents deployed, and detection content built against agreed use cases. Runbooks, severity definitions and escalation paths are written and signed off before we take the watch.

03

Operate

Analysts work the queue continuously, triaging, enriching and escalating against the agreed response times. Hunts run on cadence, and every noisy rule goes onto a tuning backlog that is worked weekly, not annually.

04

Report and Improve

Each month you get a written report and a review call covering what fired, what was escalated, what was tuned and where detection coverage still has holes, with the next quarter's priorities agreed.
