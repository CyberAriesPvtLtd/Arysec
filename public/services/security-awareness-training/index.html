Habits, Not Slide Decks

Attackers go through people because it is cheaper than going through your controls. That does not make your staff the weak link; it makes them the part of the estate that gets targeted hardest and supported least. Current tradecraft is a long way from a badly spelled bank email. Adversary-in-the-middle kits proxy a genuine login page and steal the session cookie, so a correct password and a one-time code still hand over the account. Push bombing wears down an approver until they tap accept. Callback lures avoid links entirely and route the reader to a phone number staffed by the attacker. QR codes move the click onto a personal device outside your controls. Business email compromise skips malware and simply asks a finance team to update supplier bank details, and service desk agents are talked into resetting an authenticator for someone who sounds legitimate. MITRE ATT&CK groups most of this under initial access, and none of it is defeated by telling people to look for typos.

Programme design starts from what each group actually touches. Finance and procurement learn payment verification habits: out-of-band confirmation on any change of bank details, dual approval thresholds, and a script for the call. Developers get secure coding grounded in the OWASP Top 10, the OWASP API Security Top 10 and OWASP ASVS, taught by exploiting and then repairing deliberately vulnerable applications, alongside secrets handling, dependency and build pipeline risk, and the OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications where you ship features built on models. Service desk agents work through identity verification procedure, since an agent under pressure is now a documented route to account takeover. Privileged administrators cover credential handling and jump host discipline. Directors are briefed on decisions, not technology: disclosure, notification, ransom, spokesperson, and the personal exposure that comes with being named on a company website. Privacy runs across all of it, with practical DPDP Act 2023 content on notice and consent, purpose limitation, retention and breach intimation, plus GDPR material where you handle European personal data. Delivery matters as much as content. Induction inside the first week, an annual refresher for the record and short reinforcement in between beats one long session, and teams without corporate mailboxes get toolbox talks, floor posters and messaging in the language they read instead of an e-learning link nobody opens.

Measurement is where most awareness spending loses its argument. Completion percentage is an input; it says a module was opened, not that anything changed. The numbers worth reporting are the report rate, the ratio of reports to clicks, the median time from delivery to first report, credential submission counted separately from clicking, and how the repeat-exposure group moves over successive campaigns. Those figures only mean something if the baseline is honest, so we run a first simulation before any training, hold difficulty steady long enough to see a trend, then raise it deliberately so that improvement is not simply easier lures. The reporting route gets tested as well: a report button that files into an unmonitored mailbox is worse than no button, because it teaches people that reporting achieves nothing. On the compliance side, this work carries ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 7.2, Clause 7.3 and Annex A control 6.3, the SOC 2 criteria covering competence and internal communication, PCI DSS 4.0 Requirements 12.6 and 6.2.2 where card data is in scope, and the Awareness and Training outcomes in NIST CSF 2.0. We produce the records and the mapping; the certificate itself remains a matter for your auditor or certification body.

Capabilities

What the Programme Covers

Role-Based Awareness Programme

Content mapped to what each group actually handles: finance approvals, developer code, service desk identity checks, executive exposure. Induction, annual refresher and short reinforcement between them, delivered in the languages your teams use.

Simulated Phishing Campaigns

Lures modelled on live tradecraft: credential harvesting, adversary-in-the-middle pages, attachment payloads, QR codes and callback bait. Difficulty rises as reporting improves, and every simulation ends with short teaching rather than a reprimand.

Secure Coding for Developers

Sessions built around the OWASP Top 10, the API Security Top 10 and ASVS, with hands-on exploitation and remediation in deliberately vulnerable applications, plus secrets handling, dependency risk and secure review practice.

Board and Executive Briefings

Privacy and DPDP Awareness

Practical modules on notice and consent, purpose limitation, retention and breach intimation under the DPDP Act 2023, with GDPR content where you process European personal data.

Attestation and Measurement

Policy acknowledgement tracked to named individuals across acceptable use, clean desk and AI tool rules, alongside behaviour measures: report rates, response times and repeat exposure rather than completion percentages.

How It Works

Our Engagement Process

How a Security Awareness engagement runs from first conversation to steady state.

01

Baseline

A first simulation and a short survey run before any training, so improvement is measured against your real starting point rather than a vendor benchmark. Findings are reported by cohort, never by individual.

02

Design

Roles are grouped by what they actually touch: money, code, data, access, customers. Each group gets its own curriculum, cadence and success measure, agreed with HR, legal and the business before anything is published.

03

Deliver

Induction, refresher and role modules go out on schedule, simulations run through the year at rising difficulty, and reporting routes are tested end to end so a reported message reaches someone who acts.

04

Review

Quarterly review shows movement in report rate, response time and repeat exposure by team. Weak points feed the next cycle, and the evidence pack is refreshed ahead of your audit dates.
