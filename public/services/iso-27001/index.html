What Certification Actually Requires

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certifies a management system, not a level of security. The requirements sit in clauses 4 to 10, covering context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation and improvement, and they describe a cycle you run continuously rather than a project you finish. Annex A is a reference set of 93 controls you select from, not a list to implement in full. Most nonconformities raised at certification are written against the clauses rather than the controls: a scope that cannot be justified, a risk assessment nobody repeated, an internal audit programme that never covered the whole standard.

We build the system in the order the standard depends on. Scope comes first, drawn around the services, locations, people and information systems that carry the risk your customers care about, with interfaces and dependencies stated so an auditor can see what sits outside the boundary and why. Then the risk method: criteria for acceptance and for performing assessments, identification of risks to confidentiality, integrity and availability, named risk owners, and treatment decisions recorded as modify, retain, avoid or share. The Statement of Applicability falls out of that work. It is not a spreadsheet written at the end to satisfy clause 6.1.3; it is the record of which controls you judged necessary, why the rest are excluded, and what status each one is in.

Your certificate comes from a certification body accredited by a member of the International Accreditation Forum, such as NABCB in India, UKAS or ANAB. Accreditation rules bar that body from consulting on a system it later audits, which is exactly why the implementer and the auditor have to be separate firms. Arysec is the implementer. We help you brief and compare certification bodies, prepare the scope and headcount details their quotation depends on, and sit with you through both audit stages. Where a sector obligation interacts with certification, your legal or compliance adviser should confirm what applies to you.

The ISMS You Own

Certification is the visible outcome, but what you keep is the management system itself. These are the documents and records handed over, maintained in your own repository.

  • ISMS scope statement with boundaries, interfaces and exclusions
  • Information security policy and supporting topic-specific policies
  • Risk assessment methodology and populated risk register
  • Statement of Applicability covering all 93 Annex A controls
  • Treatment plan with named owners and residual risk acceptances
  • Procedures and records mapped to the selected controls
  • Internal audit programme, checklists and first audit report
  • Management review pack, minutes and corrective action register

Capabilities

How We Build the ISMS

Scope and Context

We define the ISMS boundary against your services, sites, cloud tenancies and people, then document internal and external issues, interested parties and their requirements, including the climate change considerations added by Amendment 1:2024.

Risk Assessment Method

Your methodology is written to be repeatable: acceptance criteria, an asset or scenario based identification approach informed by ISO/IEC 27005, consistent likelihood and impact scales, named risk owners and a treatment plan they approve.

Annex A Controls

Controls are implemented across the four themes, 37 organisational, 8 people, 14 physical and 34 technological, using ISO/IEC 27002:2022 guidance for the newer ones such as threat intelligence, cloud services and data leakage prevention.

Statement of Applicability

Every one of the 93 controls carries a decision: necessary or excluded, the justification for that decision, the implementation status and the link back to the risks and legal obligations that made it necessary.

Documented Information

Policies, procedures and records are drafted to fit how your teams already work, version controlled, and kept to what clause 7.5 and the selected controls require rather than padding the system with unread documents.

Internal Audit and Review

We run the first internal audit against the full standard, raise findings with corrective actions, then prepare and facilitate a management review covering every input that clause 9.3 lists.

How It Works

Our Engagement Process

How a ISO 27001 engagement runs from first conversation to steady state.

01

Scope and Assess

We fix the ISMS boundary, document context and interested parties, then run the first risk assessment and a gap review against clauses 4 to 10 and the 93 controls.

02

Build

Policies, procedures and controls are implemented against the treatment plan. We draft what can be drafted, configure alongside your engineers where tooling is involved, and train the control owners who will run each one.

03

Operate and Audit

The system runs for a period so records accumulate. Internal audit covers the whole standard, corrective actions close, and top management holds a documented review before we declare you ready for certification.

04

Certify and Sustain

We support Stage 1 and Stage 2 on site, respond to findings, and then hand you an annual calendar for risk reviews, internal audits and the surveillance visits that keep the certificate live.

ISO 27001 — Common Questions

For a first certification with a focused scope, six to nine months is realistic; a multi-site or multi-entity scope runs nine to fifteen. The constraint is rarely documentation. It is the operating record: your certification body needs to see the risk assessment, internal audit, management review and at least two to three months of controls actually running. Stage 1 usually sits four to eight weeks before Stage 2, and certification bodies book out, so the audit dates are worth reserving early.

No. Clause 6.1.3 requires you to determine the controls necessary to implement your chosen risk treatments, compare that set against Annex A to check nothing was missed, and then justify inclusions and exclusions in the Statement of Applicability. In practice, few controls are excludable. Secure development can be excluded by an organisation that writes no software, and some physical controls fall away for a fully remote team. Exclusions written to avoid work get challenged at Stage 1, so we test each one against evidence before it goes in.

The transition window set by the International Accreditation Forum closed on 31 October 2025, and certificates issued against the 2013 edition are no longer valid. If you transitioned in time, your existing cycle continues and the 2022 requirements are audited at surveillance. If you did not, you are starting a new certification rather than an upgrade, though the existing controls, records and policies carry most of the work. We rebuild the Statement of Applicability against the 93 restructured controls and address the eleven that are new.

We can write the documents, and a system built only from documents fails Stage 2. Certification auditors interview control owners, sample records and ask people to describe what they actually do. If your access review exists as a policy but nobody performed it, that is a nonconformity regardless of how the document reads. Expect two to four hours per control owner, a half day each for the risk workshop and management review, and engineering time wherever a control needs a configuration change rather than a procedure.

Certification runs on a three-year cycle. Surveillance audits follow in the next two years, narrower than Stage 2 but focused on whether the system still operates, and recertification falls due before the third anniversary. Between visits you owe a repeated risk assessment, an internal audit covering every clause and applicable control across the cycle, a management review, and closed corrective actions. We hand this over with a calendar and templates. Clients who retain us for the annual internal audit get a team that did not build the system.
