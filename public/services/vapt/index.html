Assessment, Penetration Test, Red Team

The two halves of VAPT answer different questions, and buying one when you needed the other is the most common waste in this market. A vulnerability assessment is breadth: authenticated and unauthenticated scanning across the estate, tuned to remove noise, producing a ranked inventory of weaknesses that exist. A penetration test is depth: a tester chains those weaknesses together to establish what an attacker could actually reach, which is almost never the same list. A scanner reports that a host runs an outdated library. A tester tells you the library gave shell access, that the service account behind it was over-permissioned, and that the customer database was two steps further on.

Our testing follows published methodology rather than a tool's default profile. Network work is run to the Penetration Testing Execution Standard and the NIST SP 800-115 technical guide. Web applications are tested against the OWASP Testing Guide and OWASP Top 10, with coverage measured against an agreed OWASP ASVS level wherever you need depth you can prove rather than depth you were promised. APIs are assessed against the OWASP API Security Top 10, mobile builds against the OWASP Mobile Application Security Verification Standard. Every action we take is labelled with its MITRE ATT&CK technique identifier, which lets you replay the whole engagement against your own alert history afterwards.

Testing happens only with written authorisation. Before anything is sent to a live system we agree rules of engagement naming the in-scope addresses and hosts, the excluded systems, the test windows, the permitted techniques, the escalation contacts on both sides, and the point at which we stop. Production is treated as production: no denial of service against live traffic unless you specifically commission it in a controlled window, no destructive payloads, no mass password guessing that locks out your users, and no exploitation of anything that could affect availability without clearing it with your named contact first. Critical findings reach you the day we confirm them, not three weeks later in a report.

The Report and the Evidence

Two audiences read a penetration test report: a board that needs the risk position and an engineer who needs to reproduce the finding. We write for both.

  • Signed rules of engagement and authorisation letter
  • Executive summary with business risk and overall posture
  • Technical findings rated with CVSS v4.0 vectors
  • Reproduction steps and proof-of-concept evidence per finding
  • Remediation guidance written for the team owning the fix
  • Attack narrative showing how findings were chained together
  • Retest report confirming which issues are genuinely closed
  • Clean summary letter suitable for customers and auditors

Capabilities

What We Test

External and Internal Network

External testing works from the internet against your perimeter: exposed services, remote access and mail. Internal testing starts from a network foothold and pursues privilege escalation and lateral movement toward domain compromise.

Web Application Testing

Manual testing against the OWASP Top 10 and ASVS: broken access control, injection, authentication flaws, server-side request forgery and business logic abuse. Authenticated roles are tested against each other for horizontal and vertical escalation.

API Security Testing

REST, GraphQL and SOAP endpoints are tested from their specification: object and function level authorisation, mass assignment, rate limiting, token handling and undocumented methods the front end never calls but the gateway still exposes.

Mobile Application Testing

Android and iOS builds are examined statically and at runtime: local data storage, keychain and keystore use, certificate pinning, hardcoded secrets, tampering and root or jailbreak controls, plus the backend the app talks to.

Cloud Configuration Review

AWS, Azure and Google Cloud tenants are reviewed against CIS Benchmarks and provider guidance: identity and access policies, public storage, network exposure, key management, logging coverage and the privilege paths connecting them.

Wireless, Phishing and Red Team

Corporate wireless and guest segregation, phishing and voice pretext campaigns run under agreed rules, and objective-led red team exercises that test whether your detection and response functions notice a quiet intruder.

How It Works

Our Engagement Process

How a VAPT engagement runs from first conversation to steady state.

01

Scope and Authorise

We agree targets, exclusions, test windows, permitted techniques and escalation contacts, then take written authorisation from someone able to give it. Third-party hosted systems need their provider's consent, which we help you obtain.

02

Map the Surface

Reconnaissance and enumeration establish what is actually exposed: hosts, services, versions, subdomains, endpoints and roles. Automated scanning runs here, then every result is verified by hand so false positives never reach your report.

03

Exploit

Confirmed weaknesses are exploited within the agreed limits to prove impact, then chained: credentials reused, privileges escalated, segmentation tested, reachable data identified. Anything that risks availability is cleared with you first.

04

Report and Retest

Findings are rated, evidenced and walked through with your team, with critical issues raised as we find them. After you remediate, we retest the affected findings and reissue the report at no extra cost.

VAPT — Common Questions

Because a scanner reports conditions and a tester reports consequences. Scanners find the missing patch; they cannot tell you that the patch matters because that host holds a service account with domain rights, or that three medium findings combine into full compromise. They also miss the whole class of business logic flaws that look like normal application use, such as price manipulation, workflow bypass and access to another tenant's records. Keep the scanner for coverage and cadence. Use testing to establish what an attacker actually achieves.

That is the risk we design the engagement around. Denial of service testing is excluded by default and runs only if you commission it in a controlled window. We throttle traffic, avoid mass authentication attempts that would lock out accounts, and confirm with your named contact before exploiting anything that could affect availability. Test accounts and any files we upload are recorded and removed at the end, and the list goes in the report so your team can verify. If something behaves unexpectedly, we stop and call you.

Grey box gets you more findings for the same money. A black box test spends a large share of its hours rediscovering things you could have told us in an hour, and real attackers have time we do not. Give us documented credentials for each user role, an API specification and an architecture diagram, and testing time goes into authorisation logic and privilege boundaries instead of enumeration. Where the point of the exercise is measuring detection rather than coverage, black box makes sense, and we scope it deliberately.

Ask at scoping and you will get a direct answer. Most contractual and framework requirements, including ISO/IEC 27001:2022, PCI DSS 4.0 and SOC 2, call for testing that is technically competent and independent of the team that built the system, which our reports satisfy. Certain Indian filings instead require a tester holding a specific government empanelment. Arysec does not hold that status, and no report can substitute for it, so we check your real requirement before you commit rather than after you have paid for the wrong thing.

Annually is the common floor, driven by ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and PCI DSS 4.0 expectations, but the honest trigger is change: a major release, a new external service, a cloud migration, a merger. A report describes a point in time and starts ageing immediately, which is why we recommend pairing an annual deep test with continuous scanning and a shorter targeted test on significant releases. The included retest keeps the current report accurate once fixes land, and where a change is large enough that a retest will not cover it, we say so.
