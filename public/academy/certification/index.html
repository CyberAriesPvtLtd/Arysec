A Certificate That Records Something

Attendance Is Not an Outcome

Most training certificates record that somebody sat in a room. That is worth very little to a hiring manager, an auditor or the person who has to rely on the skill. Our certification tracks are assessed, and the certificate states what was assessed and how it was marked.

The assessment has two parts. A written paper checks that the delegate understands the standard, the law or the technique. An applied submission checks that they can produce the artefact — a scope statement, an audit finding, a rights-request workflow, a fixed and tested vulnerability.

Rubrics are published before the course starts. Nobody should have to guess what a pass looks like, and an instructor should not be able to move the line after the fact.

What the Certificate Records

Every certificate is issued to a named individual and carries a verification code your HR team or a client can check with us.

  • The programme name and the version of the standard or law it covered
  • The date of the taught course and the date of assessment
  • The components assessed — written paper and applied submission
  • The rubric version used for marking
  • A verification code, checkable by email with us
  • An expiry date three years from issue
  • The issuing entity, Arysec Academy

The Process

From Course to Certificate

Four steps, with published timings so nobody is waiting on an unknown.

01

Sit the Course

Attendance at the full taught programme is required. Assessment-only entry is not offered: the applied work depends on exercises done during the course.

02

Written Paper

Sat at the end of the taught days, typically 60 to 90 minutes. Scenario questions rather than recall, because reciting a clause number proves nothing.

03

Applied Submission

Submitted within 21 days. A real artefact built for your own organisation where possible, marked against the published rubric by an instructor who did not teach your cohort.

04

Result and Certificate

Marked within 10 working days. A pass produces a certificate with a verification code; a referral comes with written feedback and one free resubmission.

What Our Certificates Are Not

Arysec Academy certificates are issued by us. They are not issued under an accredited personnel certification scheme, and they are not equivalent to credentials from an accreditation body or a scheme owner. We will never imply otherwise, and we would rather lose a sale than let a delegate believe they hold something they do not.

For most organisations that is fine: the certificate exists to evidence competence internally, to satisfy an auditor asking about training under ISO 27001 Annex A 6.3, and to give the delegate a record of assessed work. If you need an accredited credential for a specific contractual or regulatory reason, tell us at scoping and we will say plainly whether our track is the right preparation or whether you should go elsewhere.

Several delegates each year use our courses as preparation and then sit a scheme exam separately. That is a perfectly sensible route and we will help you plan it.
