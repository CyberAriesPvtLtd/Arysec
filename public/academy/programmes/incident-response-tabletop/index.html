Plans Fail on Decisions, Not on Documents

Almost every organisation has an incident response plan. Very few have found out what happens when the person named in it is on leave, when the contact list lives on the file server that is now encrypted, or when legal and communications disagree about what to tell customers while the clock is running.

A tabletop exercise puts your actual team in a room with a scenario that unfolds over time. Injects arrive on a schedule — a journalist calls, the backup restore fails, a regulator's deadline appears — and the group has to decide, out loud, with the information they actually have.

The output is not a certificate. It is a findings report listing what broke, ordered by how much it would have cost, with an owner and a date against each item. Organisations that run these twice a year respond measurably faster, because the first thirty minutes have already been rehearsed.

What the Exercise Produces

Everything is written down as it happens, so the report reflects what the room actually did rather than what people remember doing.

  • A timeline of decisions, delays and the points where the plan stalled
  • Gaps in the plan, the contact tree and the escalation thresholds
  • Findings ranked by impact, each with a named owner and a target date
  • A tested view of who declares an incident and who can authorise spend
  • Communications drafts stress-tested against a hostile timeline
  • A record of the exercise for auditors, insurers and the board
  • A retest scope, so the next exercise measures whether it was fixed

Content

Scenarios We Run

Ransomware With Exfiltration

Encryption across file servers and a backup estate, plus a double-extortion demand. Isolation, restore decisions, the ransom conversation, regulator notification and customer messaging.

Business Email Compromise

A finance mailbox is compromised and a supplier payment has already left. Recovery, forensics, insurer and bank contact, and the awkward call to the supplier.

Data Breach and DPDP Intimation

Personal data confirmed exposed. Assessment, the intimation clock, what the notice must contain, and coordinating legal, communications and support under a deadline.

Cloud Account Compromise

A privileged identity is abused in your cloud estate. Containment without destroying evidence, blast radius assessment, and rebuilding trust in the environment.

Third-Party and Supply Chain

A supplier is breached and your data is in scope. Contractual levers, what you can and cannot compel, and communicating about an incident you do not control.

Insider Data Removal

A departing employee takes customer data. Evidence handling, HR and legal coordination, and the difference between a technical response and an employment process.

How It Runs

From Scoping to Delivery

How a IR Tabletop engagement runs, start to finish.

01

Read the Plan

We read your incident response plan, contact tree and any prior incident reports before designing the scenario, so the injects hit the assumptions you have actually made.

02

Design the Scenario

Injects are built around your systems, suppliers and regulatory position. A generic scenario produces generic findings that nobody acts on.

03

Run the Exercise

Half a day, facilitated, with a scribe recording decisions and timings. Leadership, technical responders, legal and communications all in the same room.

04

Report and Retest

A findings report inside a week, then a shorter follow-up exercise later in the year that specifically retests what was supposed to have been fixed.

Benefits

What Changes Afterwards

A Faster First Hour

The first hour sets the cost of the whole incident. Rehearsing it converts hesitation into a sequence people have already been through once.

Decisions Made in Advance

Whether you would ever pay, who can authorise emergency spend, and when customers get told are all much better decided on a Tuesday afternoon than at midnight.

Insurer and Board Assurance

Cyber insurers increasingly ask whether the plan has been exercised and when. A dated report with tracked findings is a straight answer.

Cross-Team Familiarity

Much of the delay in real incidents comes from people who have never worked together. The exercise is often the first time they meet.

Who This Is For

  • Leadership teams who would have to make the call on disclosure or ransom
