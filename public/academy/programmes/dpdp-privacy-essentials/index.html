The Act is short. The operational consequences are not. Two days on what a data fiduciary owes, what consent has to look like, how rights requests get answered inside 30 days, and what breach intimation means when the clock is already running.

The Gap Between the Text and the Build

Reading the DPDP Act takes an afternoon. Working out that your signup flow needs an itemised notice, your CRM needs a lawful-basis field, your support team needs a rights-request workflow, and your vendor contracts need processing clauses takes considerably longer — and that is the part this course covers.

We work through the obligations in the order they hit an organisation: notice and consent at collection, purpose limitation as data moves between systems, retention and erasure, data principal rights with their deadlines, breach intimation, and the additional duties that land on a Significant Data Fiduciary.

The room is deliberately mixed. Privacy failures usually happen in the gap between what legal drafted and what engineering built, so legal, product, engineering and support learn the same material together and leave having agreed who owns which obligation.

Content

Course Modules

Scope and Roles

What counts as digital personal data, who is a Data Fiduciary and who is a Data Processor, extraterritorial reach, and the exemptions that are narrower than people hope.

Notice and Consent

The itemised notice requirement, free, specific and informed consent, the deemed-consent grounds and their limits, and designing withdrawal that is as easy as giving consent.

Data Principal Rights

Access, correction, erasure, grievance redressal and nomination — with the operational reality of finding a person's data across systems inside the deadline.

Children and Vulnerable Groups

Verifiable parental consent, the prohibition on tracking and behavioural advertising directed at children, and how age assurance interacts with the rest of your product.

Security, Breaches and Intimation

Reasonable security safeguards, what triggers intimation to the Board and to affected principals, what the notice must contain, and the records to keep from the first hour.

Significant Data Fiduciaries and Vendors

The additional duties — DPO, independent audit, DPIA — plus processor contracts, cross-border transfer, retention schedules and the deletion obligations that follow.

How It Runs

From Scoping to Delivery

How a DPDP & Privacy engagement runs, start to finish.

01

Pre-Course Mapping

We ask for a rough list of the systems holding personal data and the products in scope, so the exercises are grounded in your processing rather than a generic example.

02

Day One — Obligations

The Act and the Rules, worked through in the order obligations arise, with the staged commencement dates and what has to be ready when.

03

Day Two — Operationalising

Applied workshops on notice, consent, rights workflow and breach response, ending with a written allocation of who owns each obligation.

04

Assessment

A written paper plus a short applied submission: a notice draft and a rights-request workflow, marked against a published rubric.

Benefits

What Changes Afterwards

Compliance Work That Is Correctly Sequenced

Most organisations start with a policy document. The useful order is knowing what data you hold, then notice and consent, then rights, then retention — and the course sets that order.

Fewer Product Rewrites

Consent and retention decisions are cheap at design time and expensive after launch. Getting engineering into the room early avoids a rebuild later.

A Faster Answer to Customer Due Diligence

Enterprise buyers and Indian regulators now ask directly how you handle DPDP obligations. Trained staff and drafted artefacts turn that into a short exchange.

Readiness Before Enforcement Matures

Building a rights workflow calmly is a project. Building it after the first request arrives is an incident.
