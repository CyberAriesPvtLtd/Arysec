Taught From Implementations, Not From the Standard

Plenty of courses walk you through the clauses in order. That teaches you what the standard says and leaves you unable to answer the questions that actually decide a certification: what belongs in scope, how much risk assessment is enough, and what evidence a certification body will accept for a control you have implemented differently.

This track is built around the decisions. Every module starts from a situation — a scope that keeps growing, a risk register nobody maintains, a Statement of Applicability with eleven controls marked not applicable — and works back to what the standard requires and what an auditor will do with it.

You leave with a working set of artefacts drafted against your own organisation: a scope statement, a risk assessment method, a draft Statement of Applicability, and an internal audit plan. Delegates who complete the applied assessment receive an Arysec Academy certificate recording what was assessed.

What Delegates Leave With

The applied work is done against your own organisation wherever possible, so the course output is the first draft of your ISMS rather than a worked example about a fictional company.

  • A defensible scope statement and the reasoning behind its boundaries
  • A risk assessment method with criteria, scales and acceptance thresholds
  • A populated risk register with owners, treatment and target dates
  • A draft Statement of Applicability covering all 93 Annex A controls
  • A documented information structure that does not collapse under change
  • An internal audit programme and a management review agenda
  • An Arysec Academy certificate on passing the written and applied assessment

Content

Course Modules

Scope, Context and Interested Parties

Clauses 4 and 5. Drawing a boundary that is honest, auditable and small enough to certify, and handling the interfaces and dependencies an auditor will probe.

Risk Assessment and Treatment

Clause 6. Building a method that produces comparable results across assessors, choosing scales that mean something, and writing treatment plans with owners and dates rather than aspirations.

Annex A 2022 in Practice

All 93 controls across the four themes, with focused time on the eleven new ones — threat intelligence, cloud services, ICT readiness, data masking, monitoring, secure coding and the rest.

The Statement of Applicability

Justifying inclusion and exclusion, linking controls to risks, and keeping the SoA in step with reality as controls change during implementation.

Operating the ISMS

Clauses 7 to 10: competence, awareness, documented information, monitoring, internal audit, management review, nonconformity and corrective action as a working cycle rather than a paperwork exercise.

Certification and Surveillance

What Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits actually examine, how findings are graded, what a major nonconformity costs you, and how to prepare for surveillance without an annual panic.

How It Runs

From Scoping to Delivery

How a ISO 27001 Lead Implementer engagement runs, start to finish.

01

Pre-Course Review

We ask for whatever governance material exists so the applied exercises use your real context. Organisations starting from nothing work through a supplied case study instead.

02

Four Taught Days

Roughly half instruction and half applied work, in cohorts of up to 20 so exercises can be reviewed individually. Split across two weeks if release time is tight.

03

Assessment

A written paper on the standard plus an applied submission — a scope statement, risk assessment extract and SoA rationale — marked against a published rubric.

04

Post-Course Support

Thirty days of email access to the instructor for questions that surface once implementation starts, which is when the real ones appear.

Benefits

What Changes Afterwards

An Implementation That Starts Immediately

Delegates go back with drafted artefacts rather than notes, which usually removes the first two months of an implementation.

Fewer Findings at Stage 2

The nonconformities that delay certification cluster around scope, risk method and SoA justification. Those get the most time here for exactly that reason.

Internal Capability Instead of Dependency

An organisation with two trained implementers can maintain its own ISMS between surveillance visits rather than buying consultancy every quarter.

A Common Vocabulary

When IT, legal and operations have all sat the same course, arguments about what the standard requires get much shorter.
