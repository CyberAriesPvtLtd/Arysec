Frequently Asked Questions

A vCISO performs the role of a Chief Information Security Officer on a part-time, retained basis. That covers owning your security strategy and roadmap, running the governance and policy framework, maintaining the risk register, reporting to your board or audit committee, and leading the response when something goes wrong. The difference from a consultant is accountability: your vCISO owns the outcome, not just the advice.

It depends on your jurisdiction and how you process personal data. Under India's DPDP Act, organizations classified as Significant Data Fiduciaries must appoint a DPO based in India. Under the GDPR, a DPO is mandatory for public authorities, for large-scale systematic monitoring, and for large-scale processing of special category data. Many organizations appoint one voluntarily because clients and partners increasingly expect it. We can assess your position in a short call.

Yes — the two roles are distinct and complementary. External auditors provide an independent opinion for third parties; internal audit works for management and the audit committee to test whether controls operate effectively day to day. We plan and run your internal audit programme, and our working papers are prepared to a standard that supports your external audit rather than duplicating it.

For most mid-sized organizations, six to nine months from gap analysis to Stage 2 audit is realistic, assuming reasonable internal availability. Organizations with mature IT controls sometimes move faster; those starting without documented policies or a risk process should plan for longer. We give you an evidence-based timeline after the initial gap assessment rather than a generic estimate.
